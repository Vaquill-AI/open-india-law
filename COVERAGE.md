## Tribunals and regulators

**Matters** are distinct cases on the index. **Documents on file** counts every file held for a matter, which exceeds the matter count because a forum issues several documents per case.

Read the *Reasoned decisions* column carefully. Most tribunal files are procedural order sheets and case-status records rather than substantive decisions: only about **348,516 of 2,112,201 documents (16%)** are reasoned decisions. The estimate comes from a random sample of 150 documents per forum and is accurate to roughly 8 percentage points either way; Matters and Documents on file are exact counts.

CAT CIS is blank because none of its 150 sampled documents was a reasoned decision. Its files are order sheets recording listings and adjournments, so the share is too small for the sample to measure. It is also the single largest document set here, at 1.26M files.

## Held vs embedded

Measured 2026-08-14 by a full key listing of the R2 `corpus-text/` prefix
(14,600,558 High Court objects, 174,471 Supreme Court objects) against the
embedded judgment counts above.

**These are not the same set, and the difference runs both ways.** The counts in
the Courts table are what is embedded in the vector index. They are not what is
held. Three findings matter:

- **Orissa High Court has no extracted text at all.** 283,063 judgments are
  embedded and searchable, but not one of them has a text file on record.
- **Bombay High Court has 213,214 fewer texts than embedded judgments**, the only
  other court where the index is ahead of the extraction.
- **Rajasthan High Court has 733,208 more texts than embedded judgments** -
  1,057,775 held against 324,567 embedded. Either the embedded figure is a
  severe undercount or roughly 70% of Rajasthan was never indexed. Unresolved.

Net, 1,786,868 more judgments are held as extracted text than are embedded.
A release built from the extracted text therefore covers materially more than
the vector index does.

Supreme Court objects carry per-language variants (base plus `_EN` and regional
translations), about 5.0 objects per judgment, so the 174,471 SC objects
correspond to roughly the 34,954 judgments recorded above.

## Relationship to release eligibility

This document records **what is held**. It does not record what is publishable.
Release eligibility is tracked separately in [`coverage.yml`](coverage.yml), which gates on verified source terms and a completeness audit.
A corpus can be fully covered here and still be blocked there.
